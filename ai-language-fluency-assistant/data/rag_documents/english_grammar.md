## Topic: Past Simple
Level: beginner

Use Past Simple for actions that finished in the past. Words like yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago show that the time is finished.
Form: verb + ed for regular verbs (played, worked). Irregular verbs change their form (go - went, eat - ate, see - saw, have - had).
Questions and negatives use did + base verb: Did you go? I did not go.
Examples: I went to the market yesterday. She watched a movie last night.
Common mistake: "I go market yesterday." Correct: "I went to the market yesterday." Do not use the present form with yesterday.
Common mistake: "Did you went?" Correct: "Did you go?" After did, use the base verb.
Hinglish tip: Jab kaam khatam ho chuka ho aur time bataya ho (kal, pichhle hafte), tab Past Simple use karo.

## Topic: Present Perfect
Level: pre_intermediate

Use Present Perfect for experiences, changes, and actions with a result now. The time is not important or not finished.
Form: have/has + past participle (have seen, has finished).
Signal words: ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, recently.
Examples: I have visited Goa twice. She has already eaten.
Common mistake: "I have seen him yesterday." Correct: "I saw him yesterday." Do not use Present Perfect with a finished time like yesterday.
Hinglish tip: Experience ya abhi tak ka result batana ho to Present Perfect, finished time ho to Past Simple.

## Topic: Present Perfect Continuous
Level: intermediate

Use Present Perfect Continuous for an action that started in the past and is still continuing now, or has just stopped with a visible result. It shows duration.
Form: have/has + been + verb-ing.
Use for with a length of time (for two years, for three hours). Use since with a starting point (since 2022, since Monday).
Examples: I have been working here for two years. She has been studying since morning. I have been waiting for an hour.
Common mistake: "I am working here since two years." Correct: "I have been working here for two years." Do not use the simple present with for/since.
Common mistake: "Why we use have been?" We use have been + verb-ing to show an action that began earlier and continues now.
Hinglish tip: Koi kaam pehle shuru hua aur abhi bhi chal raha hai ("do saal se kaam kar raha hoon"), to have been + ing use karo.

## Topic: Articles (a, an, the)
Level: beginner

Use a before a consonant sound (a book, a university) and an before a vowel sound (an apple, an hour).
Use a/an for one thing when it is not specific or mentioned for the first time. Use the for a specific thing, something already mentioned, or something unique (the sun).
No article is used for general plural or uncountable nouns (I like music. Dogs are friendly).
Examples: I bought a car. The car is red. She is an engineer.
Common mistake: "I am engineer." Correct: "I am an engineer." Use an article before a job title.
Common mistake: "He is a honest man." Correct: "He is an honest man." The word honest starts with a vowel sound.
Hinglish tip: Hindi mein articles nahi hote, isliye English mein a/an/the lagana yaad rakho.

## Topic: Subject-Verb Agreement (has / have)
Level: beginner

The verb must match the subject. Use has with he, she, it, and singular nouns. Use have with I, you, we, they, and plural nouns.
Examples: She has a car. They have two cars. The boy has a bag. The boys have bags.
In simple present, add s or es for he, she, it: She works. He goes. It rains.
Common mistake: "I has a car." Correct: "I have a car."
Common mistake: "He have a phone." Correct: "He has a phone."
Common mistake: "She go to school." Correct: "She goes to school."
Hinglish tip: I, you, we, they ke saath have. He, she, it ke saath has.

## Topic: Prepositions of Time (in, on, at, for, since)
Level: elementary

Use at for exact times (at 5 pm, at night). Use on for days and dates (on Monday, on 5 May). Use in for months, years, seasons, and parts of the day (in July, in 2024, in winter, in the morning).
Use for with a length of time (for two hours). Use since with a starting point (since Monday).
Examples: The class starts at 9 am. I was born in 2004. We meet on Fridays.
Common mistake: "I will meet you in Monday." Correct: "I will meet you on Monday."
Common mistake: "I live here since 2020." Correct: "I have lived here since 2020."
Hinglish tip: Time chhota to at, din ya date to on, bada period (mahina, saal) to in.
